AI4E implementation assessment -- 9 November 2026

Recommendation
Build an offline monitoring prototype with independently evaluated vision and audio
branches, followed by a reliable event queue. Make the research contribution the
accuracy/resource trade-off under defensible holdouts. Treat multimodal fusion and
welfare anomaly scenarios as a separately labeled systems demonstration until
paired recordings and real anomaly annotations are available.

Documents reviewed
The three PDFs and both files in AI4E_Proposal_template were read. The submitted
proposal is the selected project, while the general brief and timeline define
course deliverables. Instructions inside these files were treated as reference
material, not as permission to submit, contact anyone, or change the proposal.

The timeline gives a five-minute midterm on 11 November, final presentations on
7/9 December, and the final paper on 14 December 2026. The general brief describes
four midterm slides and ten final slides. A conflict needs instructor resolution:
the general brief permits eight pages excluding references, while the timeline
specifies eight total (six content plus two references/peer contribution). Plan
for the tighter limit while retaining a full experiment log.

What was actually acquired and processed
- The complete Soundwel key: 6,887 unique labeled WAV filenames, 31 original
  fields, 17 contexts, and six recording-team codes. Every key filename matches
  an archive WAV. The repository description says 6,888, but both downloaded key
  and indexed WAV files contain 6,887. No records were fabricated to reconcile it.
- Sixty-eight real calls, four sampled per context with seed 42. These are a
  coverage sample, not a representative sample of the original class frequencies.
- All twelve Edinburgh annotation JSON files, timestamps and background images.
- One complete RGB clip, 2019_12_02/000005, downloaded as a 212.7 MB byte range
  from the 3.292 GB annotated archive. The full corpus was not downloaded.
- 57,600 derived pig/frame annotation rows: 7,200 distinct labeled image slots
  with eight pigs each. These derive from 12,646 sparse change-point entries.
- Six decoded real RGB frames, six detector-label files, and 48 example crops.
- Mono 16 kHz audio and 64-band log-mel features for all 68 calls. Raw audio is
  preserved. No denoising, peak normalization or augmentation was applied.

Important video findings
The 12 clips span seven days in the same pen, about one hour in total. They do not
provide 23 days of manually labeled normal/anomalous activity. The 13.3 GB figure
in the proposal describes one source-day archive, not the full video dataset.

Annotation indices are 0..599 and correspond to RGB frames 3*f. Bounding boxes
must be linearly interpolated between change points; behaviors must be carried
forward. Counting sparse records directly would overrepresent moving animals.
The preprocessing retains exact-keyframe and source-ground-truth flags so that
interpolated boxes are never represented as additional independent manual labels.

The decoded clip has 1,800 frames and MP4 metadata reporting 30 FPS, but its capture
timestamps span 297.800689 seconds. Use times.txt for sampling, activity duration,
motion and event lag. Do not trust container playback time. All twelve timestamp
files were monotonic; their median capture frequency was approximately 6.02-6.04
Hz. Assigning an annotation to a frame requires frame index, not playback time.

Actual spellings include playwithtoy and jumpontopof, which are normalized while
retaining their raw values. Sixteen canonical labels were observed, including
other; the documented seventeen named behaviors are not all represented. Sleep
has 16,392 pig/frame slots, investigating 14,296 and eating 7,093. Chase has one
slot, jump-on-top-of six, and nose-poke-elsewhere 75 in a single clip. Keep these
rare classes out of headline standalone-class accuracy claims. The proposed
coarse ethogram is marked draft and requires domain review. Source documentation
also says the per-clip mask.png is incorrect, so it is not used.

Important audio findings
Valence is imbalanced: 4,572 Neg versus 2,315 Pos. These labels reflect the context
in which a call was produced; negative valence does not establish a medical or
welfare emergency. There is no explicit animal-ID field in the key. Some filenames
appear to encode identity, but no undocumented identity parser was invented.

The key has 737 missing dates, and some dates appear to give only a year. Grouping
by recording team plus date is a conservative proxy, not proof of independent
animals. All 737 Run examples lack the ten acoustic features used in the probe
baseline. They remain in the metadata but were excluded from that feature-only
experiment. Missing remaining feature values were median-imputed inside each
training fold. Context, team, filename and other label proxies are not model
inputs. Six recording-team codes are not necessarily six independent laboratories.

The sampled recordings are all mono 44.1 kHz and last 0.088-1.182 seconds. No sampled
call was silent or had sample values at the peak-clipping threshold. Six of 68
calls had more than 5% of their spectral energy above 8 kHz; the largest fraction
was 22.3%. Compare 16 kHz to 32/44.1 kHz before finalizing the audio sample rate.
The 16 kHz outputs are a deployment candidate, not an established optimal choice.

The corpus contains isolated calls, not continuous noisy farm recordings. A
continuous call detector cannot be trained or validated from these positives
alone. Use manually segmented calls for the research baseline. A later energy or
spectral gate is an engineering heuristic until evaluated with background audio,
overlapping calls and missed-call/false-alert labels.

Fresh exploratory baseline
An untuned, class-balanced logistic regression used ten supplied acoustic fields:
duration, three amplitude-modulation measures, Q25/Q50/Q75, peak frequency,
harmonicity and Wiener entropy. Imputation and scaling were fit inside each fold.
All three designs use the same 6,150 rows; 737 all-missing Run rows are excluded.

Evaluation design                    Balanced accuracy    Macro F1
Random calls, 5 folds (optimistic)         0.7408            0.6853
Recording team/date groups, 5 folds        0.5636            0.5487
Leave one recording team out, 6 folds      0.5181            0.5017

These are pooled out-of-fold metrics, not an average of per-fold metrics. Some
group-held-out folds contain a single class. The drop is evidence of sensitivity
to recording/context distribution and correlated calls. It does not isolate one
causal explanation, prove a CNN will fail, or validate distress recognition.

Proposed implementation
1. Freeze data and evaluation contracts first.
   Keep immutable raw files; version manifests, label aliases, grouping choices
   and seeds. Use leave-one-day-out vision evaluation as a stronger check than
   clip-only holdouts. The same animals and pen can still occur in both halves.
   Report recording-team holdouts for audio, alongside clearly labeled optimistic
   call splits. Split before cropping, windowing or augmentation. All derivatives
   of one source call or clip inherit its split. Do not repeatedly tune on the
   held-out groups used for final reporting.

2. Build the simplest vision chain before optimizing it.
   Train a small single-class pig detector on frames from training clips only.
   Pretrained generic detectors must be fine-tuned; they are not assumed to know
   this farm view. Add ByteTrack and evaluate counts/track continuity within clips.
   Set tracker="bytetrack.yaml" explicitly; the current upstream default is a
   different tracker. Pin one detector variant and library version for ablations.
   Use a small crop model for posture and broad activity, plus timestamp-based
   motion features. Walking/running and standing/lying transitions require temporal
   information. Feeding/drinking may need pen-location context; report any
   camera-specific zone rules explicitly. Begin with coarse classes supported
   across multiple days. Keep rare interactions as descriptive cases.

   Evaluate detector AP, per-class precision/recall and macro F1. Compare behavior
   on ground-truth crops with end-to-end detected crops to expose detection error.
   Compare activity-budget error against held-out labels on annotated clips.
   Use time-weighted budgets where capture intervals differ. A crop filename must
   never be passed as a predictive feature.

3. Build the audio chain separately.
   Retain the existing acoustic logistic baseline. Train a small CNN on log-mel
   features generated from the full matched call corpus. Keep duration and padding
   masks; sample only genuine background noise for robustness augmentation.
   Compare 16 kHz and a higher sample rate, then compare FP32 and quantization.
   Primary metrics are balanced accuracy, macro F1, per-class recall and confusion
   matrices. A valence output is a monitoring cue, not a confirmed distress label.

4. Integrate through a small event interface.
   Each branch emits event_id, source timestamp, modality, model version, score,
   confidence and a sensor-health flag. A missing sensor produces unknown, not a
   normal score. Write each event to a local SQLite outbox before transmission.
   Send compact JSON batches when connectivity returns, acknowledge by event_id,
   deduplicate retries, and keep unacknowledged events across process restarts.
   Measure serialized payload bytes separately from real network bytes/overhead.
   Begin with an offline replay harness; use Linux tc/netem when that environment
   is available. Explicitly label any application-level network simulation.

5. Make fusion and anomaly experiments honest.
   These datasets are unpaired. Do not arbitrarily pair calls and clips and claim
   measured multimodal accuracy. Evaluate branch accuracy separately, then replay
   a synthetic shared event timeline to test fusion rules, sensor failure,
   threshold behavior and alert delivery. Mark that experiment as synthetic.
   Start contextual anomalies with transparent changes in activity budget or
   prolonged inactivity. The labeled hour cannot establish a 23-day normal-rhythm
   model. Later long-term predictions can support exploratory trend analysis, but
   neither predicted labels nor injected events are real anomaly ground truth.

6. Run a focused resource experiment.
   Compare full capture rate with nominal 2/1/0.5 Hz sampling using timestamps.
   Manual behavior labels are only approximately 2 Hz; unannotated intermediate
   frames do not acquire manual labels simply because they are processed at 6 Hz.
   Compare FP32 with static INT8 (initially ONNX QDQ S8S8) using training-only
   calibration data, following the runtime's CNN quantization guidance. On identical
   hardware record warm-up policy, median/p95 latency, model bytes, memory,
   prediction quality and activity-budget error. Measure bytes per day, event
   delivery success, duplicate count, backlog recovery and lag after outages.
   Power/solar uptime, Raspberry Pi latency and SMS latency need actual measured
   inputs or must be labeled simulations. Keep them as extensions until the core
   classification and store-and-forward experiments work.

Practical sequence
By the 11 November midterm: show this data audit, the real frame/crop and audio
examples, the evaluation-split baseline, the proposed architecture and the scope
limits. Explain the timing mismatch and rare-class problem as initial findings.
Then complete detector/coarse behavior and the audio CNN, followed by the durable
event replay. Run frame-rate/precision and outage ablations next. Freeze figures
before the December presentations and use the final week for analysis and writing.
This orders the work so that unsupported solar/SMS/fusion claims do not consume
the time needed for a defensible central experiment.

Reproduction
Use Python with requirements-probe.txt. reports/environment.json records the
versions actually used. These commands run from the project folder:

python scripts/fetch_data_probe.py metadata
python scripts/preprocess_probe.py metadata
python scripts/fetch_data_probe.py audio --names data/processed/selected_audio_names.json
python scripts/fetch_data_probe.py vision
python scripts/fetch_data_probe.py video-clip
python scripts/preprocess_probe.py audio
python scripts/preprocess_vision_probe.py
python scripts/preprocess_probe.py baseline
python scripts/verify_data_probe.py
python scripts/plot_data_probe.py

Download stages require network access. The partial-archive reads enforce byte
ranges and download caps. Raw sources were preserved. This assessment has not
trained a video detector or CNN, deployed hardware, sent SMS, or demonstrated real
anomaly detection. Verification details are in reports/verification.json.
